# Act One: Founder Operator

Act 1 is the opening chapter of the game.

The player starts as the founder of a tiny independent air service with one small aircraft, limited money, and minimal credibility. The airline is not yet a true scheduled carrier. It is a fragile operation trying to prove that it can reliably move passengers, cover costs, and survive long enough to become legitimate.

Act 1 should feel personal, grounded, and small-scale.

The player is not managing an empire yet. They are trying to make one aircraft, one route, and one shaky business plan work.

## Act 1 Core Fantasy

“I have one aircraft, one home base, and just enough money to prove this can become a real airline.”

## Act 1 Design Goals

Act 1 should:

- Teach the player the basic game loop
- Make aircraft choice matter immediately
- Introduce the map as the world
- Introduce simple scheduling
- Introduce fixed simulation pace and pause/resume
- Introduce the inbox as the story system
- Introduce Maya Reyes as the co-founder/advisor
- Introduce reports as feedback
- Introduce private contracts as side opportunities
- Introduce basic money pressure
- Introduce basic aircraft condition
- Lead naturally into Scheduled Commuter status

Act 1 should not:

- Overwhelm the player with the full simulator
- Introduce partner airlines yet
- Introduce hub mechanics
- Introduce large fleets
- Introduce advanced finance
- Introduce complex competition
- Allow unlimited scaling

## Main Character Role

The player is the founder of the airline.

The game does not need to define the player’s personality. The player’s decisions define the airline.

The airline itself is treated as the player character. Its reputation, cash, routes, aircraft, and relationships are the story state.

## Co-Founder / Advisor

Maya Reyes is the co-founder and early advisor.

Maya is practical, grounded, and focused on survival. She helps explain mechanics, but she should feel like a business partner, not a tutorial mascot.

Maya communicates primarily through the Inbox.

Maya should not appear as constant floating tips or speech bubbles.

## Maya Reyes: Character Direction

Maya is the person who helps turn the founder’s idea into an actual operation.

She understands that the airline needs to be boring before it can be ambitious.

Tone:

- Practical
- Calm under pressure
- Friendly but not overly bubbly
- Direct
- Occasionally dry
- Focused on survival, reliability, and credibility

She should sound like someone who knows the difference between a dream and an operation.

## Act 1 Starting Setup

At the beginning of Act 1, the player chooses:

- Airline name
- Starting airport
- Starter aircraft package
- Difficulty
- Simulation pace

The player begins with:

- One small aircraft
- Limited cash
- Very limited route authority
- No partner airline
- No advanced business systems
- No scheduled commuter certificate
- Low reputation
- Basic inbox access
- Basic map access
- Simplified route planning
- Simplified schedule board

## Starter Aircraft Choice

The player chooses between two founder aircraft.

### Aster A-8 Courier

A cheaper 8-seat founder aircraft.

Strengths:

- Lower acquisition cost
- Lower lease cost
- Lower short-term risk
- Good for cash-starved starts
- Useful for thin routes

Weaknesses:

- Lower capacity
- Lower comfort
- Lower reliability
- Limited growth potential

Best for:

- Players who want to preserve cash
- Very cautious early expansion
- Ultra-thin starter routes

### Kestrel K-10 Trail

A more capable 10-seat founder aircraft.

Strengths:

- More seats
- Better rugged airport performance
- Better reliability
- Better early credibility

Weaknesses:

- Higher acquisition cost
- Higher lease cost
- Slightly higher operating cost

Best for:

- Players who want reliability
- Slightly stronger early routes
- Faster path toward credibility

Both starter aircraft should be viable.

The choice should teach that aircraft decisions are strategic, not cosmetic.

## Act 1 Progression Limit

Act 1 should allow freedom, but it should not allow unlimited growth.

Possible Act 1 limits:

- Maximum 1 aircraft at first
- Maximum 1 scheduled route at first
- Later maximum 2 scheduled routes
- Only founder and commuter-scale aircraft
- Only Local Field, Community Airport, and limited Regional Airport access
- No partner contracts
- No hub mechanics
- No large fleet tools
- No advanced finance systems

The player can keep operating in Act 1, earn money, complete private contracts, and optimize the small operation.

To grow meaningfully beyond this scale, the player must pursue Scheduled Commuter status and enter Act 2.

## Act 1 Story Arc

Act 1 follows this arc:

1. The airline is created.
2. Maya pushes the player to keep the first route simple.
3. The player chooses a first route.
4. The player builds a first schedule.
5. The first flight or first operating period runs.
6. The player reviews the first report.
7. The player proves repeatability.
8. Private contracts appear.
9. The player learns to watch money and maintenance.
10. The player proves enough reliability to apply for Scheduled Commuter status.
11. The player chooses when to move to Act 2.

## Act 1 Objective Chain

### Objective 1: Choose the First Route

The player chooses one eligible route from the starting airport.

Purpose:

Teach route planning and map interaction.

Requirements:

- Select an eligible destination
- Confirm the first route

Reward:

- Unlock first scheduling objective
- Inbox message from Maya about keeping the operation simple

### Objective 2: Build the First Schedule

The player schedules at least one round trip on the first route.

Purpose:

Teach that aircraft time matters.

Requirements:

- Assign the starter aircraft
- Schedule at least one round trip
- Avoid schedule conflicts

Reward:

- First operating period becomes available
- Inbox message from Maya about running the clock

### Objective 3: Run the First Operating Period

The player resumes the simulation clock and lets the first scheduled flight or operating period complete.

Purpose:

Teach fixed-pace simulation and pause/resume.

Requirements:

- Resume simulation
- Complete the first scheduled operation

Reward:

- Operations report unlocked
- Route performance summary unlocked

### Objective 4: Review the First Report

The player reviews the first operations report.

Purpose:

Teach feedback loops.

Requirements:

- Open the operations report
- Review passengers, revenue, costs, and reliability

Reward:

- Reliability tracking unlocked
- Multi-day operation objective unlocked

### Objective 5: Make It Repeatable

The player operates several simulated days with no canceled flights.

Purpose:

Teach consistency and stable operations.

Requirements:

- Operate at least 3 simulated days
- Avoid canceled flights
- Maintain basic reliability target

Reward:

- Operations catch-up unlocked
- Maya inbox message about stable schedules

### Objective 6: Let the Schedule Run

The player uses operations catch-up or continues operating the same stable schedule over multiple days.

Purpose:

Teach that stable operations generate progress while the player is away.

Requirements:

- Complete a catch-up report or operate enough stable time
- Maintain positive or near-positive operating performance

Reward:

- Private contracts unlocked

### Objective 7: Take Work Where You Can Get It

The player accepts and completes a small private contract.

Purpose:

Teach optional side objectives.

Requirements:

- Accept one private contract
- Complete its requirements

Reward:

- Business/finance view unlocked
- Cash reward
- Small reputation reward

### Objective 8: Watch the Numbers

The player reviews revenue, expenses, and cash runway.

Purpose:

Teach business pressure.

Requirements:

- Open business/finance view
- Review route profitability or cash flow

Reward:

- Aircraft condition and maintenance view unlocked

### Objective 9: Keep the Aircraft Healthy

The player handles or acknowledges early aircraft maintenance needs.

Purpose:

Teach that aircraft are assets that wear down.

Requirements:

- View aircraft condition
- Complete or schedule required basic maintenance if needed

Reward:

- Expanded route planning
- Second route permission

### Objective 10: Prove Scheduled Reliability

The player operates a small 1-2 route schedule while meeting reliability and cash requirements.

Purpose:

Prepare for Scheduled Commuter status.

Requirements:

- Operate for a set number of simulated days
- Maintain reliability above target
- Carry required passenger count
- Maintain minimum cash reserve or avoid emergency cash state

Reward:

- Commuter certification application unlocked

### Objective 11: Apply for Scheduled Commuter Status

The player chooses whether to continue operating in Act 1 or apply for Scheduled Commuter status.

Purpose:

Give the player control over story pacing.

Requirements:

- Meet certification requirements
- Choose to apply

Reward:

- Act 2 transition

## Act 1 Private Contract Examples

Private contracts are optional side opportunities.

They should be small, grounded, and useful.

### Weekend Shuttle Contract

A nearby city or community needs extra passenger service for a weekend event.

Requirements:

- Operate a set number of round trips
- Complete before deadline
- Maintain minimum reliability

Reward:

- Cash
- Small reputation boost

### Community Trial Service

A small airport wants to test whether regular passenger service is viable.

Requirements:

- Operate service for several days
- Carry minimum passenger count
- Avoid cancellations

Reward:

- Cash
- Improved airport relationship
- Possible future route opportunity

### Medical Travel Support

A local organization needs reliable short-hop passenger transport.

Requirements:

- Operate limited flights
- High reliability requirement
- May pay better than normal passenger service

Reward:

- Cash
- Operational trust

### Small Business Shuttle

A local business needs temporary travel support between two nearby cities.

Requirements:

- Operate specific days or time windows
- Maintain schedule reliability

Reward:

- Cash
- Business reputation

### Tourism Weekend Package

A tourism office wants weekend service to a small destination.

Requirements:

- Operate flights during a short window
- Carry minimum passengers

Reward:

- Cash
- Leisure demand boost on related route

## Act 1 Inbox Message Examples

### Welcome Message

Sender: Maya Reyes  
Subject: We are officially doing this

We have the paperwork, the aircraft, and just enough money to make this either brilliant or embarrassing.

Do not start big. Start repeatable. One short route, one aircraft, one schedule we can actually keep.

If we can prove people will trust us with the simple stuff, the bigger opportunities will come later.

Action: Open Route Planning

### First Schedule Message

Sender: Maya Reyes  
Subject: Keep the first day boring

One aircraft means one bad schedule can ruin the day.

Give yourself enough time on the ground. The goal is not to squeeze every possible minute out of the airplane yet. The goal is to operate tomorrow too.

Action: Open Schedule Board

### First Report Message

Sender: Maya Reyes  
Subject: First day in the books

That was not glamorous, but it was real.

Review the report before changing anything. Passenger count, revenue, costs, and reliability matter more right now than expansion.

Action: Open Operations Report

### Catch-Up Unlock Message

Sender: Maya Reyes  
Subject: The schedule can run without us hovering over it

We have enough of a routine now to let the operation run while we step away.

Catch-up will keep stable scheduled flights moving, but it will not make major decisions for us. If something important happens, we stop and deal with it.

Action: View Catch-Up Info

### Private Contracts Message

Sender: Maya Reyes  
Subject: Small work still pays

We are starting to get requests that do not fit normal scheduled service.

Some of them are awkward. Some are barely worth the fuel. Some are exactly what keeps the lights on.

Private contracts are not the dream, but they can buy us time.

Action: Open Contracts

## Act 1 Completion

Act 1 ends when the player proves the airline can operate reliably enough to pursue Scheduled Commuter status.

The player should not be forced to transition immediately.

When ready, the player can apply for Scheduled Commuter status and move into Act 2.

The game should present this as a strategic decision, not an automatic level-up.
