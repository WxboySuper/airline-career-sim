# Career Storyline

The career storyline is the main vertical trunk of the game.

The player is free to operate outside the storyline, but the storyline gives the game structure, purpose, unlocks, and long-term direction.

The story should feel like an open-world progression game. The player chooses when to move forward, when to prepare, and when to stay in a stage longer.

## Story Structure

The story is divided into acts.

Each act represents a major phase in the airline's growth. Acts unlock new mechanics, aircraft classes, airport types, contracts, and business pressures.

Story progression should happen through:

- Career objectives
- Strategic decisions
- Contract completion
- Operational performance
- Reputation growth
- Financial stability
- Partner relationships

The story should not progress automatically during operations catch-up. Major story decisions wait for the player.

## Act 1: Founder Operator

The player starts as the founder of a tiny independent air service.

They have one small aircraft, limited money, minimal credibility, and a small number of possible routes. The airline is barely a business yet.

The player may be operating alone or with a small co-founder/advisor presence.

### Theme

Prove the operation can exist.

### Player Fantasy

“I have one aircraft, a fragile plan, and a chance to turn this into a real airline.”

### Core Mechanics

- Create an airline
- Choose a starting airport
- Acquire or begin with one founder aircraft
- Open a tiny route
- Build a simple schedule
- Run flights
- Earn revenue
- Pay operating costs
- Maintain aircraft condition
- Complete early career objectives
- Use operations catch-up for stable scheduled flights

### Story Pressure

Nobody trusts the airline yet. The player needs to prove reliability before larger opportunities appear.

### End Goal

Qualify for scheduled commuter operations.

## Act 2: Scheduled Commuter

The airline becomes a small scheduled carrier.

The player now operates repeatable routes between small airports, community airports, and regional airports.

### Theme

Reliability creates credibility.

### Player Fantasy

“We are not just flying people around anymore. We are becoming an actual airline.”

### Core Mechanics

- Scheduled route authority
- Multiple routes
- Small fleet growth
- Basic staff costs
- Basic facility costs
- Maintenance expectations
- Early recurring contracts
- Stronger route demand logic
- Early reputation effects

### Story Pressure

The airline must prove it can operate consistently, not just survive.

### End Goal

Attract attention from a larger partner airline.

## Act 3: Regional Partner

A larger fictional airline offers a regional partnership.

The partner provides money, credibility, better routes, aircraft access, airport access, and passenger feed. The partnership is extremely helpful early on.

### Theme

The ladder appears.

### Player Fantasy

“This partnership could turn us from a fragile commuter operation into a real regional airline.”

### Core Mechanics

- Partner offer
- Partner contract
- Partner routes
- Reliability requirements
- Guaranteed revenue or subsidies
- Partner reputation boost
- Partner restrictions
- Contract penalties
- Partner-controlled opportunities

### Story Pressure

The airline can grow faster with help, but that help comes with strings.

### End Goal

Complete the first major partner contract and unlock affiliate growth.

## Act 4: Affiliate Growth

The airline grows under the partner relationship.

The player can remain in this stage as long as they want, building money, reputation, aircraft, routes, and operational experience.

### Theme

The ladder becomes a cage.

### Player Fantasy

“We are bigger and safer now, but not fully free.”

### Core Mechanics

- Partner dependency
- Expanded partner routes
- Partner-owned or partner-financed aircraft
- Larger regional aircraft
- Restricted route freedom
- Brand growth limitations
- More demanding contracts
- Early competitor pressure

### Story Pressure

The player begins to notice that the partnership limits long-term ambition.

### End Goal

The player becomes eligible to explore independence, but is not forced to do so.

## Act 5: Independence Path

The player decides when to explore independence.

This should be user-driven. The game may suggest independence, show readiness indicators, or create pressure, but it should not force the player to break away.

### Theme

Freedom has a price.

### Player Fantasy

“We might be ready to survive under our own name.”

### Core Mechanics

- Independence assessment
- Strategic decision panel
- Asset ownership review
- Route ownership review
- Buyout costs
- Partner penalties
- Revenue shock
- Reputation changes
- Transition objectives

### Possible Choices

- Stay partnered
- Deepen the partnership
- Negotiate independence
- Hard break from partner

### End Goal

Become a self-sufficient regional airline.

## Act 6: Self-Sufficient Regional

The player now operates under their own brand with more freedom.

The airline can build its own network, develop hubs, compete more directly, and prepare for national expansion.

### Theme

The airline belongs to you now.

### Player Fantasy

“We are no longer just operating someone else’s feed. This is our airline.”

### Core Mechanics

- Independent route network
- Basic hubs
- Connecting demand
- Competitor pressure
- Larger regional aircraft
- Crossover jets
- Business upgrades
- Stronger brand/reputation effects

### End Goal

Qualify for national carrier expansion.

## Future Acts

Future updates may expand the storyline into:

- National airline growth
- Major hub development
- Continental international routes
- International partnerships
- Long-haul operations
- Alliances
- Airline group/subsidiary management
- Airport partnership projects
- Global aviation empire gameplay
