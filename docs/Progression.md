# Progression

Progression is the system that connects the story, simulator, unlocks, and player freedom.

The player should always have meaningful direction, but should not feel trapped in a rigid tutorial path.

## Progression Types

### Career Objectives

Career objectives are the main story progression tasks.

They guide the player through the airline’s growth and unlock new systems, aircraft classes, airport types, contracts, and strategic decisions.

Examples:

- Operate your first route
- Complete your first scheduled day
- Maintain reliability above a target
- Carry a certain number of passengers
- Reach stable profitability
- Qualify for scheduled commuter operations
- Complete the first partner contract

Career objectives should be visible, understandable, and tied to the main storyline.

### Contracts

Contracts are optional side objectives.

They give the player extra money, reputation, unlocks, or opportunities. Some contracts are simple and recurring. Others are larger strategic opportunities.

Examples:

- Weekend event shuttle
- Small-town commuter subsidy
- Regional feeder route
- Medical travel support
- Corporate shuttle
- Seasonal tourism service
- Partner airline feeder contract

Contracts should give the player something useful to do outside the main story path.

### Milestones

Milestones are long-term achievements.

They reward growth but do not necessarily control story progression.

Examples:

- Carry 1,000 passengers
- Carry 10,000 passengers
- Operate 5 aircraft
- Operate 10 routes
- Maintain high reliability over 30 days
- Reach a cash reserve target

### Strategic Decisions

Strategic decisions are major story or business forks.

They should be rare, meaningful, and player-driven.

Examples:

- Accept a partner airline offer
- Deepen the partnership
- Explore independence
- Negotiate breakaway
- Hard break from partner
- Commit to a new hub strategy

## Unlock Philosophy

Unlocks should represent growing capability.

The player should unlock new opportunities because the airline has proven it can handle them.

Unlocks may be based on:

- Reliability
- Passenger volume
- Cash reserves
- Profitability
- Reputation
- Contract completion
- Aircraft experience
- Route count
- Airport class access
- Story act completion

## Rewards

Progression can reward the player with:

- Cash
- Reputation
- Operational trust
- New aircraft classes
- New airport classes
- New route types
- New contract types
- Partner offers
- Business upgrades
- Strategic decision availability
- Cosmetic options

Rewards should support growth without trivializing the business challenge.

## Player Pace

The player controls the pace.

A player may stay in an act longer to:

- Save money
- Complete contracts
- Improve reliability
- Build reputation
- Acquire aircraft
- Optimize schedules
- Prepare for the next story step

The game can suggest next steps, but should not force progression unless the player has chosen a scenario with strict rules.

## Challenge Pressure

The game should create pressure through simulation systems, not arbitrary timers.

Pressure can come from:

- Operating costs
- Lease payments
- Aircraft condition
- Contract deadlines
- Reliability requirements
- Limited cash
- Airport limitations
- Partner restrictions
- Competitor behavior

## Progression Anti-Goals

Avoid:

- Giving the player large aircraft too early
- Unlocking systems before they matter
- Forcing story progression during catch-up
- Making contracts feel like random chores
- Making progression only about money
- Using fake mobile-game currencies as the main progression driver
